設計與族群

研究設計randomized double-blind longitudinal active-comparator trial; no non-tea control
族群71 adults aged 18–70 with metabolic syndrome, recruited in Haidian District, Beijing; mean age 53.5 years.
樣本量71
茶類黑茶
具體茶品Liupao tea aged 1, 4, 7, or 10 years

介入與比較

介入形式one sachet of Liupao tea brewed as a beverage each day
茶飲／萃取物／單體brewed tea beverage
劑量6 g/day; 250 mL boiling water in a 300 mL teapot; steeped 30–45 seconds and re-steeped until the liquor faded
時長90 days
對照組No dedicated non-tea or placebo control; four active groups compared Liupao tea aged 1, 4, 7, or 10 years.

結局與結果

主要結局Blood pressure; lipid and glucose measures; body weight and body composition; gut microbiota, assessed at baseline and post-intervention.
次要結局Appetite and liver-health biomarkers were reported as additional measures.
結果The paper reports several within-group pre/post changes and no significant intergroup differences in clinical parameters or body-composition regulation across tea-aging groups. Because every group received Liupao tea and there was no non-tea control, the independent effect of tea cannot be determined.
不良事件Daily follow-up was designed to monitor adverse events and instructed affected participants to stop tea and contact researchers; an aggregate adverse-event count was not identified in the extracted main text.
統計顯著性Several within-group comparisons were reported at p<0.05; between-group comparisons for clinical and body-composition outcomes were not significant (p>0.05).
臨床重要性Not established; the study lacked a dedicated non-tea control and did not define a clinical-effect threshold.

限制與透明度

限制No dedicated control group; diet and physical activity were not systematically tracked. Time effects and other confounding cannot be excluded, so observed changes cannot be attributed to Liupao tea alone.
資助The article states that it received no external funding; study tea samples were supplied free of charge by Guangxi Zhaoping County General Mountain Agricultural Technology Co., Ltd.
利益衝突The authors declared no conflicts of interest; the tea-sample supplier is disclosed separately.

Background : Metabolic syndrome (MetS) represents a significant global health challenge. Liupao tea (LPT), a post-fermented dark tea, has shown potential metabolic benefits, but clinical evidence remains limited. Objectives : This study aimed to investigate the effects of LPT with varying aging durations on clinical parameters, body composition and gut microbiota in individuals with MetS. Methods : In a randomized, double-blind trial, patients with MetS were randomly assigned to intervention groups, receiving 6 g/day of LPT aged for 1, 4, 7, or 10 years, respectively, over a 90-day intervention period. 原文段落 · 2.1 1. Introduction信心 0.6

Metabolic syndrome (MetS) refers to a cluster of clinical conditions associated with multiple high-risk metabolic factors or diseases. The World Health Organization (WHO) defines MetS as a pathological state characterized by the clustering of chronic metabolic diseases. It is also known as Syndrome X [ 1 ]. The global prevalence of MetS is rising, driven by urbanization, sedentary lifestyles, and dietary changes [ 2 ]. In China, the overall prevalence of MetS is 22.0%, with 23.6% in females and 21.0% in males. Urban areas report a prevalence of 23.5%, while rural areas have a slightly lower rate at 20.3% [ 3 ].…
