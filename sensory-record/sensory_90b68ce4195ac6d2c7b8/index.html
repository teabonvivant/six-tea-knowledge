Foam formation impairs tea beverages' sensory quality and stability, necessitating defoaming. This study examined the effects of a silicone defoamer (10–50 mg/kg) on the aroma of oolong tea beverages via sensory evaluation, electronic nose ( E -nose), headspace solid-phase microextraction gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPM E -GC–MS), and molecular docking. Results showed that 10 mg/kg sufficed for defoaming and yielded the highest sensory scores. Two E-nose sensors were sensitive to aroma changes. The test sample, Wuyi rock tea made from the Rougui variety of oolong tea, was purchased from the Wuyishan Tea Market (Nanping City, China). Sodium chloride (chromatography grade) was obtained from Merck KGaA (Darmstadt, Germany). Ethyl decanoate (chromatographic grade) and C 7 -C 40 n-alkanes mixture (chromatographic grade) were supplied by Shanghai Anpu Experimental Technology Co. Ltd. (Shanghai City, China). The food-grade silicone emulsion defoamer (Foamdoctor® F3011), containing 20% active polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), was provided by Penn White Ltd. (Middlewich, Cheshire, United Kingdom).

Fifteen evaluators (7 males and 8 females, aged between 20 and 50 years) were selected from the faculty and students of the Tea Science major at Fujian Agriculture and Forestry University.
