The flavor profile of tea, characterized by sweetness, mellowness, freshness, bitterness, and astringency, is formed by water-soluble compounds, including amino acids, polyphenols, caffeine, soluble sugars, and tea pigments. Caffeine, a prominent alkaloid, confer a characteristic bitterness that intensifies the perceived strength of the tea. Of the many flavor compounds, amino acids, which are key contributors to the umami and sweet taste of tea, are typically more abundant in spring tea.
